Slow checkout

About 53 seconds, silent, in landscape, portrait and square. One real problem from start to finish: checkout got slow this week. Opus 5.5 finds three suspects and asks to check each one in its own thread. You click Start. The three threads work at once, their answers come back to Opus one by one, and the last one names the cause. Opus fixes it.

  • Why this story: "check three things at once" is the job people already split by hand, opening three chats and pasting results back. Here the agent does the splitting and the answers come back to it.
  • Why this bug: a page that got slow needs no code to understand, and the cause fits in one line: one database query per item in the cart.
  • What it has to show that subagents don't: these are real threads. They sit in the sidebar, each has its own worktree, and you can open one and read along.

Decided (Will, Oct 6)

  • The story: approved as written.
  • Mixed models: payments on Sonnet 5.5 at High, database queries on GPT-6.1-Sol at Extra High, page size on Opus 5.5 at Extra High. Sol's thread is the one we peek into and the one that finds the cause, so a different provider makes the key find, as Astra did in the 0.5.0 video. The request box shows only model names; the reasoning level shows in each thread's own chat. Opus, the thread you talk to, stays at High.
  • The peek: kept.

Portrait and square

At the tall take's width the sidebar is a drawer, and the header keeps the parent's name but drops the words "Started by". So the tall and square takes open the drawer to show the three threads, tap one, and rely on its first message ("Checkout got slow · request") to show who started it, then go back through the drawer. If that runs long, those two formats skip the peek.

What Sol said (v1 → v2)

  • The story contradicted itself: if the checkout API answers in 40 ms, the slow cart queries can't be behind it. The first suspect is now the payment service, which really is separate.
  • The screens were off in three places: Opus's row shows a cyan "3 threads" with a clock (not the word "Waiting"), working threads have blue dots, and finished threads stay listed with a grey check. Fixed.
  • Scene 3 was too tight: the second answer couldn't get its reading time. Sol suggested cutting Opus's short lines, but they're real (Opus takes a turn after every answer), so I gave the scene 3 more seconds instead. The video is about 53 s.
  • "Any signed-in agent" was too broad: agents billed per use can't take a thread. Reworded.
  • The tall take needs its own path through the sidebar drawer (above), and the recorder needs more work than "play four threads" (below).

What it takes to record

The recorder from 0.5.0 plays one scripted thread through the real app, with no agent running and no usage spent. This story needs more from it:

  • Script the parts a background service does in the real app: setting up the three threads and their worktrees, and delivering each answer to Opus.
  • Play four threads at once and keep all of them scripted when the camera moves from one to another. Today, opening another thread loads its real state and would wipe the script.
  • Play the request box and your click on Start.
  • New camera paths and labels for the sidebar, and the drawer path for the tall take.

The edit, the title cards and the export from last time carry over.

Rules for the edit

  • Every line is plain English, about 12 words or fewer.
  • The key line holds 4 s and the others 3 s after they finish, with the camera still. Opus's two short lines hold about 1.5 s.
  • Captions say the point of the scene in plain words.
  • One label at a time, and never over text being read.
  • Every step is something the app really does.
