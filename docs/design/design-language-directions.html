A · Canvas & panels: the work sits on a soft raised panel, settings come in quiet grouped blocks

The window frame becomes the darkest layer and the sidebar sits right on it. The page you're working in (a settings page, a thread, the side panels) is a rounded panel lifted one step lighter. Inside settings, each section is a softly tinted block with no border. Every page gets a real title. The colors already exist in the app: it has a five-step ladder of greys that today's screens barely use.

What it feels like

  • Calm and current: close to where Linear, Figma and the newer desktop AI apps have landed.
  • Depth without decoration: layers come from shades of grey, not shadows or borders.

Will it age?

  • Well: it's how real surfaces stack, not a trend effect.
  • No blur, glow or gradient to fall out of fashion.

Across the app

  • Thread, side panels, settings, usage, pull requests all become panels on the same frame.
  • Lists inside panels use the tinted block; menus float one step lighter again.

Cost and risk

  • Medium: mostly shared layout and a few building blocks. The colors already exist.
  • The panel edge costs about 8px of width on each side.
The system in three rules

Three heights

Frame (darkest) holds the sidebar. Panels hold the work. Blocks group things inside a panel. Menus and popups float above.

framepanelblockmenu

Three corner sizes

12px for panels and the composer, 10px for blocks inside a panel, 7px for buttons, inputs and chips. Nothing else.

Type does the rest

Page title 20px. Section title 13px in normal case (no tiny all-caps labels). Row title 13.5px, description 12.5px grey. One accent blue, only for "on", "selected" and the main button.

B · Ledger: two columns, led by type, almost no surfaces

Each section gets a left column with its name and one line about it, and the settings sit to the right on thin lines. It uses the empty width that today's page wastes, and reads like a well-set document. In a thread it stays flat, with crisp lines and an outlined composer.

D · Glass: see-through panels, pill shapes and soft glows

Panels let a deep tinted backdrop show through. Controls become pills, "on" states glow, and corners get bigger. It's the most striking of the four, and the closest to Apple's 2025 "Liquid Glass" look.

  • Showy, premium at first sight.

Will it age?

  • Fastest of the four. Glass and glow are this year's trend.

E · Quiet workspace: option A's grouped blocks inside option B's continuous workspace (Sol's recommendation)

No floating panels. The thread is one continuous reading area, and the sidebar and side panels are docked on a slightly lighter surface with a single dividing line. Depth only shows up where it means something: grouped settings, the composer (where you type), and menus floating on top. Type is a size larger and grey text never drops below comfortable contrast. Chat text is 15px in a column about 72 characters wide.

  • Long reading stays calm: no rounded bands stacking up through a 50-message thread.

F · Quiet native: E's calm layout with C's controls, and no rainbow

The structure is E: one continuous reading area, docked side panels, quiet grouped settings. The controls come from C: solid grey buttons and dropdowns with a faint top highlight and a small shadow, macOS-style up-and-down arrows on dropdowns, the white-knob switch, and text fields that look slightly pressed in. G · Quiet workspace with native form controls: one system, two weights of control

The rule that makes E and F one system: controls inside a form (the settings rows) are solid and pressable, because they sit next to text and need to be found. Controls inside a toolbar or list (the sidebar, the message box, the panel headers) stay plain until you hover, because their area already frames them. Icons never get boxes. This is how macOS itself works: toolbar buttons are borderless, settings dropdowns are filled.

Solid (form controls)

  • Settings dropdowns, buttons, switches and text fields.
  • Dialogs and setup screens too: anywhere you fill something in.

Plain (toolbar controls)

  • Sidebar, message box, panel headers, menus: no box until you hover.
  • The thread sidebar stays exactly as today: inbox rows, Wrapped section, soft fill on the open thread.
  • In the settings menu the selected page's icon turns blue.

Icons

  • Always plain glyphs, one grey, never in tiles.
  • Color only for provider logos and real status.

Why it holds together

  • Same greys, same corners, same type everywhere; only the control weight changes, and it changes for a reason you can state.
